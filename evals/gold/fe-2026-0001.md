The GDM mechanistic interpretability team tested whether sparse autoencoders (SAEs) help on a downstream task that can be described without mentioning interpretability: training probes for harmful user intent that still work on new jailbreaks and new datasets. They expected probes on one or a few SAE latents to generalise better out of distribution than a dense linear probe; instead the dense probe was near-perfect on every split, while SAE probes (including ones from SAEs finetuned on chat data) fell short out of distribution. The authors read this as evidence against SAEs being the right tool for this kind of task, say they cannot tell which of several explanations applies, and say they will deprioritise fundamental SAE research while not calling SAEs useless.
