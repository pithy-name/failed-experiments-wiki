## Where it went wrong
- **fe-2026-0004** — failure type `hypothesis_false` (result direction `null`): the hypothesis was that treating SAE features as the primitives, gradient-based edge-level circuit discovery would give a concrete, algorithmic explanation of GPT-2-small's next token on a naturalistic forward pass, with each node and edge adding to the story. The reasons the entry gives:
  - Only two of the five top proximal features seemed to call the model's top next token.
  - The strongest upstream edges were inspected for just two prompts, and were not illuminating even for those.
  - The authors' interpretation is that local approximations from vanilla SAEs probably cannot be strung into fully explanatory circuits, and that cross-layer approximations are probably needed. The entry says this view is their interpretation and they did not test it.
  - The entry flags its own limits: five prompts, whether circuits "explained" anything was judged by reading graphs with no quantitative criterion, the SAEs used and the number of top nodes kept per sublayer are not stated, and no bug audit is described beyond fixing a few bugs during the replication.

## What to avoid
- Do not judge "explains the forward pass" by reading a handful of graphs. The only attempt in the wiki had no stated quantitative criterion, which weakens both its negative result and any positive result you would get the same way. Fix the criterion before you run (from fe-2026-0004's limitations and revival conditions).
- Do not assume the largest feature node at the output position accounts for the model's predicted token. In that attempt only two of five did (from fe-2026-0004).
- Do not treat a passing sanity check as evidence that the method works on ordinary text. The Copyright (C) sanity check passed in that attempt, yet the naturalistic prompts still did not yield explanations (from fe-2026-0004).
- Be careful about starting from vanilla, per-sublayer SAEs as your feature basis. That is the setup the authors call local approximations and the one that fell short. The authors' alternative (cross-layer approximations) is untested by them, so it is a lead, not a result (from fe-2026-0004).
- Record the details the entry leaves out, so your run is comparable: which SAEs, how many top nodes per sublayer, the bug audit (from fe-2026-0004's limitations).
- Aim your claim at the scale the evidence supports. The only attempt used five short prompts, with upstream edges inspected for two. A claim of a "full circuit" for ordinary text goes far beyond anything the wiki shows tested (from fe-2026-0004's claim scope).
