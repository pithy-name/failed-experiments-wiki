## Where it went wrong
- **fe-2026-0001** — failure type `hypothesis_false`: the SAE probes did not generalise better than the dense probe. The entry quotes the source: "on the OOD set, SAE probing does not match the performance of a linear probe". It does not identify a cause. The authors read the result as evidence against SAEs being the right tool for this kind of task, and say they cannot tell which of several explanations applies. The entry names known SAE problems, such as missing concepts and feature absorption, as things a retry should address. Its stated limits: single model, layer and concept, and one train vs out-of-distribution split of jailbreak types. The text gives no AUROC or other scores for the single-latent and k-sparse probes, because they appear only in plots. The setup may be too noisy to separate the SAE finetuning variants. The authors used SAEs to clean spurious formatting differences out of the data before training any probe, which they say slightly stacks the deck against SAEs because the dense probe benefited too.
- **fe-2026-0008** — failure type `unknown`: the author did not know why the sparse encodings under-performed. They listed three open possibilities: high-level features are missing from the sparse code, sentiment in particular is poorly encoded, or the hidden-state pipeline contains a mistake. The entry says it is not established whether transformer-lens handles left padding and batch processing correctly, so a pipeline mistake is not ruled out. The SAE reconstruction errors were larger than the author expected, though another researcher confirmed that the squared-error values matched their own.

## What to avoid
- Don't assume a handful of SAE latents will beat a dense residual-stream probe on unseen jailbreaks. The one wiki test of that hypothesis found the dense probe near-perfect on every split and the SAE probes short of it out of distribution (from fe-2026-0001). Treat your hope as already contradicted for that setup, not as untested.
- Test on whole held-out jailbreak types and whole datasets, as that entry did with two held-out jailbreaks, two held-out rewrites and other datasets, and keep an in-distribution validation set alongside (from fe-2026-0001).
- Check your harmful vs harmless prompt sets for incidental differences, such as formatting, that a probe could pick up instead of intent. That entry's authors cleaned spurious formatting differences out of their data before training any probe. If you clean with SAEs, the dense baseline benefits too, which can understate the SAE probes' standing, so report it (from fe-2026-0001).
- Before reading a gap as an SAE failure, check your SAE reconstruction quality and your hidden-state extraction (left padding, batching). In the small-model entry the reconstruction errors were larger than the author expected and the padding and batch handling was not confirmed correct, so a pipeline bug could explain its gap (from fe-2026-0008).
- Don't treat either entry as settled. Both are unreviewed author reports, and fe-2026-0008 is agent-written with low confidence (from fe-2026-0001 and fe-2026-0008).

## Worth retrying
- **fe-2026-0001** — `retry_welcome` is true. Revival conditions: retry with SAEs that address known problems such as missing concepts or feature absorption, with a larger probing dataset, on other models, layers or concepts, or where labels are very noisy or data are scarce. The entry says related work cited in the post found sparse probes can win in those last two cases. The authors say they would return to SAE research given significant progress on core SAE issues or a particularly important and tractable direction. They also suggest attention-head probes would do well here but do not report testing them.
- **fe-2026-0008** — `retry_welcome` is true and confidence in the negative is low. Revival conditions: retry if the left-padding and batch handling of the hidden-state pipeline is confirmed correct, or if the same comparison is run on tasks other than sentiment.
