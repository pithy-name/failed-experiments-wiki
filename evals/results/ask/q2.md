## Already tried
- **fe-2026-0002** — One author re-implemented the Jacobian Lens (J-Lens) on CPU and compared it with the Logit Lens on GPT-2 small and GPT-2 medium. Each lens was scored on the rank of the model's own top-1 next token at each layer (Metric A, 60 held-out prompts). It was also scored on the logit-minus-J gap for tokens 1 to 5 positions ahead (Metric B, measured at layer 9 in the headline test). J-Lens lost at all 11 layers evaluated on small. On medium it won at 1 of 23 layers, by a margin the post calls rounding error. The entry says five follow-up stress tests did not change this. The post's own conclusion is "J-Lens does not work on GPT-2 small or medium", but only within the scope below.
  Models: GPT-2 small (124M), GPT-2 medium (355M) · Evidence: author_report, confidence it is a true negative: medium · Badges: unreviewed, retry-welcome. The contributor note says this is a model-written draft, awaiting maintainer verification and not yet human-verified.
  Scope the entry gives: this is a clean re-implementation, not the official code. J was fitted on 30 sequences (the entry reads this as the J fitting set), and 150 in one refit. The original paper used about 1000. Nothing was tested on larger GPT-2 models, other model families or instruction-tuned models.

## Where it went wrong
- **fe-2026-0002** — failure type `hypothesis_false`: the J-Lens did not beat the Logit Lens at recovering the model's next-token prediction, or tokens a few positions ahead, from earlier layers. The scaling test also found no J-Lens advantage emerging with model size, but that rests on two model sizes, which the post itself calls one data point.
  The entry gives these reasons and caveats:
  - The post cautions that rank metrics can be inflated by token frequency.
  - The post also cautions that magnitude thresholding can surface outlier dimensions instead of structure. A sparsity-thresholded J-Lens beat the Logit Lens at 7 of 11 layers on Metric A at the 1% keep setting. The post attributes this to the metric rewarding frequent filler tokens and to GPT-2's outlier dimensions, not to real structure.
  - The entry's limitations list a small fitting set (30 sequences, 150 in one refit, against about 1000 in the paper) and no run of the official implementation on the same prompts. The port was checked only with an identity-J test and a float64 finite-difference gradcheck.
  - Only two metrics were used, and skip_first was tried at 0, 16 and 32 only.

## What to avoid
- Do not read this as "Jacobian-based lenses do not work". The entry covers one re-implementation on GPT-2 small and medium, with two metrics. Report your own result with the same scope limits (from fe-2026-0002).
- If you score lenses by the rank of the top-1 token, check whether the result tracks token frequency. The post warns that rank metrics can be inflated by frequent filler tokens, and the entry lists a token-frequency correlation check among those it ran (from fe-2026-0002).
- Do not treat a win from a magnitude-thresholded or sparsified J as evidence for the lens. In this entry, a thresholded J-Lens won at 7 of 11 layers, and the post attributes that to filler tokens and GPT-2's outlier dimensions, not to real structure. Check this before you claim an advantage (from fe-2026-0002).
- Do not fit J on a few dozen sequences if you want a fair test. The entry fitted on 30 sequences (150 in one refit) against about 1000 in the original paper, and lists this among its limitations (from fe-2026-0002).
- Do not rely on a re-implementation alone. The entry validated its port only with an identity-J test and a float64 gradcheck, and did not run the official implementation on the same prompts. Run the official implementation as a cross-check, or accept that the same doubt will apply to your result (from fe-2026-0002).
- Do not expect the headline comparison to move with simple tweaks. The entry says five follow-up stress tests did not change the outcome. Its listed checks include a refit on 150 sequences, bias subtraction, sparsity thresholding, a skip-first sweep and a scale sweep (from fe-2026-0002).

## Worth retrying
- **fe-2026-0002** — `retry_welcome` is true. The entry's revival conditions say it is worth retrying:
  - with J fitted at the original paper's data scale (about 1000 sequences);
  - with the official implementation run on the same prompts as a cross-check;
  - on larger GPT-2 models (gpt2-large, gpt2-xl), or on other model families where the Logit Lens is weaker. The post notes that an improved lens in the Tuned Lens paper's appendix clearly won only on OPT-125m, not on GPT-2 medium;
  - or on an instruction-tuned model.
