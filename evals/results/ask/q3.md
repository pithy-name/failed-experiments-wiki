## Where it went wrong
- **fe-2026-0003** — failure type `unknown`: the entry does not establish why the effect did not appear. The author could not explain why the raised alignment-faking rate that Hughes et al. report for this condition did not show up, and offers three unconfirmed guesses: a difference in the prompt file, this specific checkpoint, or the HarmBench prompts chosen. Listed limitations: runs of 20 and 50 are well below Hughes et al.'s own n of 200; scoring was by hand, not with the published classifier; the with-training-details system prompt has not been checked against Hughes et al.'s exact file; the probe was not run. The scope is one checkpoint, two small runs, and the entry says it does not show this checkpoint cannot alignment-fake.
- **fe-2026-0005** — failure type `hyperparams_unexplored`: only doses within plus or minus 2 were run, and the steering was gentle (KL about 0.001, output fully coherent), so larger doses up to the coherence boundary were not tried. Also one 8B model, one prompted sandbagging setup, one difference-of-means direction, no SAE features. The author calls the results preliminary, and a higher-dose sweep could firm up the null or overturn it.

## What to avoid
- Do not assume this kind of checkpoint will alignment-fake once you add training details to the system prompt. In fe-2026-0003 it did not, and the author's own section title says getting the model to alignment-fake "was the hard part". Plan for that step to take most of the effort, and for the probe test to wait on it (from fe-2026-0003).
- Do not size the elicitation check at 20 to 50 prompts. The author says that is enough to conclude the rate is low but not to tell near zero from somewhat above zero; Hughes et al. used 200 (from fe-2026-0003).
- Check your system prompt against Hughes et al.'s exact file before you read anything into a null. fe-2026-0003 never did this check (the author lists it as future work), and a prompt-file difference is one of the author's three unconfirmed guesses for the failure (from fe-2026-0003).
- Do not rely on hand-scoring scratchpads alone. fe-2026-0003 scored by hand against a four-part definition, and its revival conditions call for running the published classifier on the saved scratchpads (from fe-2026-0003).
- Be careful about what a probe is picking up. In fe-2026-0005, a direction that separated eval from deploy framing did not move the sandbagging behavior and did not beat random directions, and the author concludes it tracks the framing. That entry is about sandbagging and steering, not alignment faking or detection probes, so treat it as a caution about confounding a probe with the monitored/unmonitored framing, not as a finding about your setup (from fe-2026-0005). That entry used a format-controlled minimal-pair dataset and a random-direction null as checks.
