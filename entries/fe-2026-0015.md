title: Training on open-ended self-explanation reports gave inconsistent generalization to a held-out
  hint setting in two Qwen models
research_area:
- interpretability

hypothesis: The authors wanted to ask a model why it behaved as it did and get a complete answer that
  names causes nobody had thought to test. They hoped that training on open-ended reports built from one
  general source of counterfactual investigations would carry over to a different, held-out setting where
  a model should mention a hint in the prompt more often when the hint really changed its answer.
claim_scope: Training target was a structured report per investigation of a model behavior on in-the-wild
  WildChat prompts, giving the behavior, its main causes, the experiments supporting each cause and any
  hypotheses that did not hold up. Evaluation covered only the hint setting. A hint such as a suggested
  MMLU answer or a user opinion on an Am I The Asshole post was placed in the prompt, the model was asked
  why it gave its answer, and a judge extracted whether the explanation named the hint as influential.
  A good explainer should name the hint more often when removing it actually flipped the answer. The post
  names two training models, Qwen3-8B and Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, but the hint paragraph itself names only
  the larger model. The only notable gain reported is 0.59 to 0.68 for the larger model (the post does
  not name the metric). Untrained models were near chance because they rarely mention the hint. The post
  gives no training set size, no hint dataset size, no training details and no numbers for the smaller
  model. It does not cover other open-ended evaluations, and nothing here shows that open-ended self-explanation
  training cannot work.

The authors treat a complete, open-ended answer to why did you do that as the long-term goal, and say it is not yet working reliably. In the same post, training on a binary counterfactual-prediction target improved every model in every setting they tested.
